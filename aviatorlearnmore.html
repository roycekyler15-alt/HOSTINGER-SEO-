Aviator looks like a game of quick reflexes. In reality, it's a game of pre-decided targets and discipline.

Players who treat Aviator as a "click fast" game lose. Players who decide their cash-out point before the round begins — and stick to it — do better.

Here's how the multiplier curve actually works on DM Play's Aviator, and how to approach it.

How Aviator Actually Works

Each Aviator round starts at a multiplier of 1.00x. As the round runs, the multiplier grows — 1.10x, 1.50x, 2.00x, 5.00x, and so on.

At any moment during the round, the multiplier can crash — the round ends, and any players who haven't cashed out lose their stake.

Your job is simple to describe but hard to execute:

Cash out before the crash, but not so early that you leave money on the table.

The crash point in every round is randomly determined. There's no signal, no warning, no pattern. The plane can crash at 1.01x or 100x — and every round is independent.

Understanding the Multiplier Curve

The multiplier curve starts flat (slow growth in the first few seconds) and then accelerates. The longer a round runs, the faster the multiplier climbs.

Here's the important thing to internalize:

  • Low multipliers are common — most rounds crash below 2.00x
  • High multipliers are rare — rounds above 10x happen but not often
  • Very high multipliers are very rare — 100x+ rounds are outliers

This distribution is by design. It means that "waiting for the big one" is statistically expensive — you'll lose many small stakes waiting for a large payout that may not come for hundreds of rounds.

The practical takeaway: decide your target multiplier before you play, and stick to it. Don't change the target mid-round because "it feels like it's going to go higher."

Cash-Out Timing Explained

Every Aviator round has one key decision: when to hit the cash-out button.

There are two ways to approach it:

  • Manual cash-out — you press the button when you decide
  • Auto cash-out — you pre-set a multiplier; the system cashes out automatically if reached

Auto cash-out is a game-changer for discipline. It removes the emotional decision. You decide your target once, before the round starts, and the system handles the rest. If the crash happens before your target, you lose. If the multiplier reaches your target first, you win.

Most experienced Aviator players use auto cash-out exclusively. It eliminates the "should I hold a bit longer?" trap that costs players money.

Common Aviator Mistakes

The most expensive mistakes in Aviator aren't about the game — they're about behaviour:

  • Changing your target mid-round — the "one more second" trap
  • Chasing losses with bigger bets — the fastest way to drain a bankroll
  • Playing without auto cash-out — relying on reflexes instead of pre-decided targets
  • Increasing stakes after wins — one hot streak doesn't change the odds
  • Playing for hours without breaks — decision quality drops after 30 minutes
  • Believing "crash prediction" content — no method predicts crashes; the outcomes are random

If any of these describe your behaviour, treat it as a signal to reset — smaller stakes, shorter sessions, stricter limits.

Risk vs Reward at a Glance

Every Aviator session is a trade-off between payout size and hit frequency. Here's a simple way to think about it:

  • Low target, high frequency — Win small amounts often. Suits cautious players.
  • Medium target, moderate frequency — Balanced approach. Suits most players.
  • High target, low frequency — Win big occasionally, lose often in between. Suits risk-tolerant players.

None of these is "correct" — but you should pick one and stick with it. The players who lose most are the ones who switch strategies after every loss.

The one rule that matters: Never bet more than you can afford to lose. Aviator is entertainment, not income.
